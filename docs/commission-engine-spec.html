Host Agency Commission Engine

A technical specification for calculating, explaining and reconciling airline commission across two contract layers — carrier → host agency, and host agency → sub‑agent — from a ticket document to a settled payout.

§1 — Scope

What the system does

One engine answers the same question from two sides of the same ticket: how much commission is owed, by whom, to whom, and on what authority?

The host agency holds contracts with carriers. Each contract sets a commission rate that varies by market, booking class, fare type and date. Today someone reads a few thousand tickets a week and computes those rates by hand, then chases the carrier for what was not paid. That is the first job.

Beneath the host sit sub-agents, each with their own agreement — a share of the host's commission on commissionable business, and a service fee on business that carries none. A sub-agent needs the same answer for their own ticket, before and after the host's cut. That is the second job, and it is the same arithmetic run one layer down.

In scope

  • Per-ticket commission calculation with a complete, human-readable audit trace
  • Two contract layers, where the sub-agent layer derives from the carrier layer rather than duplicating it
  • Bulk reconciliation of a settlement period against expected commission, with a variance work queue
  • Full document lifecycle — void, refund, exchange/reissue, ADM/ACM — with clawbacks posted as reversing entries
  • Ingestion from ARC reporting, GDS feeds, print-stream capture, ticket PDFs and manual entry
  • Statement generation and payout for sub-agents

Explicitly out of scope for v1

  • Booking, ticketing or any write path into a GDS. This system reads and computes; it never issues.
  • General accounting. It produces the commission ledger and exports; it is not the agency's general ledger.
  • Fare pricing or availability. It reads what a ticket was priced at, not what it could have been.

§2 — Domain

The money on a ticket

A ticket total is never one number, and commission is never a percentage of it. The total decomposes into a base fare plus an itemised stack of taxes and fees, each carrying a code. The distinction that decides most of the money is between government taxes and carrier-imposed surcharges:

CodeWhat it isCommissionable?
—Base fare — the filed fare itselfYes, almost always the sole basis
YQ / YRCarrier-imposed surcharge. Set by the airline, not a government.Contract-dependent. Usually excluded; some contracts include it. Single biggest error source.
USUS international transportation taxNo
ZPUS domestic flight segment taxNo
XFUS passenger facility charge (airport)No
AYUS security service feeNo
IL, GB, …Foreign government taxes and airport chargesNo
XTAggregation of several small taxes — must be exploded back to components on ingestNo
Design consequence

Taxes are stored as an itemised array keyed by code, never as a single total_tax figure. A contract that makes YQ commissionable is then a one-line change to the rule's basis, not a schema migration — and a ticket ingested before that contract existed can still be recomputed correctly.

What actually selects a commission rate

A contract clause is a set of conditions and an outcome. The conditions that appear in real carrier agreements:

  • Validating carrier — the plating carrier on the ticket (LY = numeric code 114), not the operating carrier of any segment
  • Point of sale and point of commencement
  • Market — usually a geography pair (US→IL, transatlantic, domestic), occasionally a specific O&D. Rarely a literal city pair.
  • Booking class (RBD) — inclusion or exclusion lists, often banded
  • Fare basis — matched by pattern, with exclusion patterns for promotional and group fares
  • Tour code — presence indicates a private or net fare, which normally carries nil commission
  • Fare type — published / private / group / consolidator
  • Two independent date axes — date of issue and date(s) of travel, each with its own window
  • Passenger type — ADT / CHD / INF, with infants commonly excluded

Outcome shapes

  • percent of a named basis (base fare, or base + YQ)
  • percent with a cap or a floor amount
  • flat amount per ticket or per coupon
  • nil — zero commission, which is a positive assertion, not an absence of rule
  • plb — a productivity-linked bonus or override, target-based and often retroactive, accrued per ticket but settled at period end

§3 — Data

Canonical ticket model

Every ingestion source, from a structured ARC file to a scanned PDF, normalises into one shape. Nothing downstream knows or cares where a ticket came from. Fields marked required are the minimum for a defensible calculation; a ticket missing one is queued for enrichment rather than calculated on a guess.

packages/schema — Ticket (abridged)
TicketDocument {
  ticket_number      "114-2401234567"   required  // 3-digit carrier + 10
  document_type      TKT | EXCH | RFND | VOID | EMD | ADM | ACM
  conjunction_of     string | null      // >4 coupons spill to a second stock
  validating_carrier "LY"               required
  iata_number        "05512345"         // issuing agency
  issue_date         Date               required
  pos_country        "US"               required
  currency           "USD"              required

  base_fare          Money              required  // integer minor units
  equiv_fare_paid    Money | null       // when sold in a second currency
  taxes              TaxItem[]          required  // { code, amount }, XT exploded
  total              Money              required

  fare_calc          string             // linear construction; source for prorate
  tour_code          string | null      // presence ⇒ net fare
  ticket_designator  string | null
  fare_type          published | private | group | consolidator
  pax_type           ADT | CHD | INF | ...
  reported_commission Money | null      // what the agent claimed, if known

  coupons            Coupon[]           required
}

Coupon {
  n                  1..4
  origin, dest       "JFK", "TLV"
  marketing_carrier  "LY"
  operating_carrier  "LY"
  flight_number      "LY 028"
  rbd                "C"
  fare_basis         "CRTUS"
  departure          Date
  status             OK | VOID | RFND | USED | EXCH

}
Known hard problem

A multi-coupon ticket under a market-scoped rule forces a choice: does the rule match on the ticket-level origin and destination, or per coupon with the base fare prorated between them? The fare calculation string is the only real source for a prorate. This is decided per contract via an explicit scope: "ticket" | "coupon" field, never inferred — see Q1 in §14.

§4 — Rules

Contract and rule model

Contracts are stored as data, versioned and effective-dated. They are never compiled into conditionals. A carrier that changes eight points to six next quarter is a new rule version with a new effective window — and every ticket already calculated under the old version keeps its old, correct answer.

Layer 1 — carrier to host agency

rule — carrier layer
{
  "id": "LY-US-IL-J-2026H1",
  "layer": "carrier_to_host",
  "contract_id": "ct_ly_host_2026",
  "version": 3,
  "priority": 700,
  "scope": "ticket",

  "effective": {
    "issued_between": ["2026-01-01", "2026-06-30"],
    "travel_between": ["2026-01-01", "2026-12-31"]
  },

  "match": {
    "validating_carrier": "LY",
    "pos_country":        "US",
    "market":     { "from": "US", "to": "IL", "direction": "either" },
    "rbd":        { "in": ["J", "C", "D", "I"] },
    "fare_basis": { "matches": "^[JCDI]", "not_matches": "(PROMO|GRP|IT)" },
    "tour_code":  { "absent": true },
    "fare_type":  { "in": ["published"] },
    "pax_type":   { "in": ["ADT", "CHD"] }
  },

  "award": {
    "kind":     "percent",
    "rate":     "8.00",
    "basis":    ["base_fare"],        // YQ deliberately absent
    "rounding": { "mode": "half_up", "dp": 2 }
  },

  "source": {                        // the audit link back to paper
    "document": "LY-HOST-2026.pdf",
    "clause":   "§4.2(a)", "page": 7,
    "extracted_by": "ai", "approved_by": "u_412", "approved_at": "2026-01-08"
  }
}

Layer 2 — host agency to sub-agent

The critical modelling decision. A sub-agent's seven points are expressed relative to the carrier rule that fired, not as a standalone eight-becomes-seven constant. When the carrier moves to six, the sub-agent automatically moves to five with no rule edit and no silent overpayment.

rule — sub-agent revenue share
{
  "id": "SA4471-LY-DEFAULT",
  "layer": "host_to_subagent",
  "subagent_id": "sa_4471",
  "priority": 500,

  "match": { "validating_carrier": "LY" },

  "award": {
    "kind":  "share_of_upstream",
    "mode":  "points",           // "points" | "fraction" | "absolute"
    "points": "7.00",            // of whatever the carrier rule awarded
    "when_upstream_nil": "no_share"
  }
}
rule — sub-agent service fee on nil-commission business
{
  "id": "SA4471-FEE-DOM",
  "layer": "host_to_subagent",
  "subagent_id": "sa_4471",
  "priority": 600,

  "match": {
    "market": { "from": "US", "to": "US" },
    "upstream_commission": "nil"     // only where the host earns nothing
  },

  "award": {
    "kind": "fee", "amount": "10.00", "currency": "USD",
    "per": "ticket", "direction": "debit_subagent"
  }
}

A sub-agent agreement is therefore an ordered set of these two kinds of rule, plus optional absolute overrides where a sub-agent genuinely negotiated a flat rate independent of the carrier deal.

§5 — Evaluation

Rule resolution and the pipeline

01IngestSource adapter reads the file, feed or document as-is.
02NormaliseMap to the canonical ticket. Explode XT. Validate required fields.
03MatchSelect the winning carrier rule, then the sub-agent rules.
04ComputeBasis → commission → share → fees, in integer minor units.
05PostAppend immutable ledger entries with a full trace.
06ReconcileMatch against what was actually paid; raise variances.

Resolution order

  1. Filter to rules whose effective windows contain both the issue date and the relevant travel dates.
  2. Filter to rules whose match block is fully satisfied. Every declared condition must hold; an absent condition matches everything.
  3. If more than one survives, the highest priority wins. On a tie, the rule with more declared conditions wins — specificity.
  4. If a tie remains after specificity, the engine does not choose. It emits AMBIGUOUS, names both rules, and queues the ticket for a human.
  5. If nothing matches, it emits NO_RULE and queues. It never falls back to zero silently, because a silent zero is indistinguishable from a correct nil.
Non-negotiable

A wrong confident number is worse than a queued one. AMBIGUOUS and NO_RULE are first-class outcomes with their own queues, not error states to be smoothed over.

Every calculation carries its trace

The result object stores the rule id and version that fired, each condition with the ticket value it was tested against, the rules that were considered and why they lost, the basis composition, the arithmetic at each step, and the engine version. This is what turns "our system says $149.80" into a claim that survives a carrier dispute six months later.

The second ticket is the case that justifies the whole nil-versus-missing distinction. The fee is owed because the host earned nothing — so the fee rule must be able to read the outcome of the layer above it, and a ticket that merely failed to match any carrier rule must never trigger it.

§7 — Lifecycle

Document lifecycle

The clean issue is the easy case. Manual reconciliation collapses on what happens afterwards. A ticket is therefore not a row — it is a lifecycle: one ticket number owning a chain of documents, against which commission entries are appended and never mutated.

EventCommission effectEngine behaviour
VoidNever earnedPost a full reversing entry. The original entry stays visible in the ledger; the net is zero.
Full refundClawed back in fullReverse at the original rate and rule version, not today's.
Partial refundPro-rata clawbackReverse on the refunded portion of the base. The cancellation penalty is retained revenue and is normally non-commissionable.
Exchange / reissueNet of what was already recognisedCompute commission on the new document's base, then subtract commission already recognised on the exchanged coupons. Never re-pay on carried-over base.
ADMCarrier debitPost a debit entry linked to the ticket and, where the sub-agent caused it, a matching pass-through per their agreement.
ACMCarrier creditPost a credit and re-evaluate any open variance on that ticket.
Rule

Amounts are never updated in place. Every correction is a new, dated, reversing entry that references the entry it corrects. The current position is a sum over the ledger, so any figure the system has ever shown can be reproduced for any past date.

§8 — Input

Ingestion adapters

One adapter interface, one output type: (raw) => TicketDocument[]. Adapters are independently testable against captured real samples and carry no knowledge of contracts or commission.

SourceFidelityUseNotes
ARC IAR exportHighestWeekly bulk reconciliationItemised taxes, tour code, reported commission. The reconciliation backbone.
GDS feedHighNear-real-timeSabre / Amadeus / Travelport. Per-coupon detail and fare calc string present.
Print-stream captureHighContinuous, passiveBack-office interface records captured off the ticket/invoice print stream (Server Pro). Fixed-position parsing — needs a real sample capture to specify.
Ticket PDF / e-mailMediumSingle-ticket lookupThe sub-agent "what's my cut" flow. Layout varies by issuer; low-confidence fields are surfaced for confirmation, never guessed.

The model is settlement-agnostic by construction. ARC is the v1 target; a BSP HOT-file adapter is another implementation of the same interface, and the only settlement-specific logic outside the adapters is the period calendar.

§9 — Reconciliation

Expected against actual

Reconciliation is a set difference over a settlement period: every ticket the engine expected commission on, matched against what was actually received or claimed. Everything that does not match cleanly becomes a typed work item with a reason code, an owner and an age.

ReasonMeaningTypical resolution
UNDERPAIDCarrier paid less than the contract requiresGenerate a claim with the trace attached
BASIS_MISMATCHVariance equals the rate applied to YQCarrier computed on a different basis — the highest-value pattern to detect automatically
RATE_MISMATCHPaid at a different percentageEither a contract version we do not hold, or a genuine underpayment
UNCLAIMEDExpected, never claimed, period closingEscalate before the carrier's claim window expires
CLAWBACK_DUERefunded or voided, commission still standingPost reversal; recover from sub-agent if already paid out
AMBIGUOUSTwo rules tied on specificityHuman decides; the decision amends the contract's priorities
NO_RULENo contract clause covers this ticketEither a gap in our contract data or genuinely nil — must be asserted either way

BASIS_MISMATCH deserves particular attention: when the variance on a ticket equals the contract rate applied to the YQ amount, the carrier and the agency disagree about the commissionable basis. Detected across a period rather than a ticket, that pattern is usually worth more than every other variance type combined.

§10 — Storage

Data model

Postgres. Three groups of tables: what happened (documents), what we agreed (contracts), and what is owed (ledger). The first two are inputs; the third is derived, append-only, and reproducible from the first two by replaying the engine.

TableHoldsMutability
ticketsLifecycle head, keyed by ticket numberStatus only
documentsEach TKT / EXCH / RFND / VOID / ADM against a ticketImmutable
couponsPer-segment detail, incl. prorated baseImmutable
tax_itemsOne row per tax code per documentImmutable
contractsCarrier and sub-agent agreements, with the source PDFMetadata only
rulesVersioned, effective-dated clausesNew version, never edit
calculationsOne per (document, layer, engine version), with the trace as JSONBImmutable
ledger_entriesSigned amounts by party, referencing the entry they reverseAppend-only
settlementsPeriod boundaries and what the carrier or ARC actually reportedImmutable once closed
variancesOpen work items with reason code, owner and ageStatus and notes

Money representation

Every amount is a bigint of minor units plus an explicit currency code. No floating point appears anywhere in the money path. Where a ticket is sold in a second currency, the FX rate and its date are stored on the entry, so a restated rate never silently rewrites history.

§11 — System

Architecture

A TypeScript monorepo. The engine is the asset; everything else is delivery. Keeping it pure and dependency-free is what makes the standalone product embeddable in other systems later without a rewrite.

repository layout
packages/
  engine/      pure, zero-dependency, deterministic
               no I/O, no clock, no randomness — same inputs, same outputs,
               forever. This is the publishable artefact.
  schema/      zod schemas + inferred types: Ticket, Rule, Calculation
  parsers/     one adapter per source → TicketDocument[]
apps/
  api/         Fastify + Postgres. Thin. Owns persistence and auth,
               never arithmetic.
  web/         the existing Vite/React console
fixtures/
  golden/      real tickets + hand-verified expected outputs
  contracts/   redacted contract excerpts + their extracted rules

Where AI is allowed, and where it is not

AI reads a contract PDF and proposes structured rules, each annotated with the clause and page it came from and a confidence level. A human reviews and approves before a rule can ever fire; an unapproved rule is inert. AI also assists PDF ticket parsing, flagging low-confidence fields for confirmation.

AI never performs arithmetic and never selects a rule at calculation time. The calculation path is deterministic code with golden tests. That boundary is what makes the output defensible.

§12 — Assurance

Correctness strategy

"Solid" is not a quality of the code, it is a property that has to be continuously demonstrated. Four mechanisms:

1 · Golden fixtures

Real tickets paired with the amounts the manual team already computed. A contract does not go live until the engine reproduces every fixture under it exactly. This is also the migration proof — it demonstrates the system agrees with the humans before anyone is asked to trust it.

2 · Property tests

Invariants that must hold for every generated input, not just the examples:

  • Sub-agent share plus host spread always equals the carrier commission, to the minor unit
  • Ledger entries for any ticket always sum to its current position
  • Void, then recompute, always nets to zero
  • Issue → exchange → refund never recognises more commission than a direct issue at the final fare
  • No commission is ever computed on a tax code not named in the rule's basis

3 · Determinism and replay

The engine takes no clock and no randomness; effective dates come from the ticket. Every calculation is stamped with an engine version, so the full history can be replayed after a fix and the diff reviewed before anything is restated.

4 · Explainability as a test surface

Every result renders as the §6 waterfall with clause citations. If the explanation cannot be read and checked against the contract by the person who does this manually today, the calculation is not finished — regardless of whether the number is right.

§13 — Delivery

Ordered so that something is usable and verifiable early, and so the riskiest unknown — real data — is confronted before the volume features are built on top of it.

Output — versioned API + package

§14 — Decisions

Open decisions

These change the schema or the engine, so they are worth settling before Phase 1 rather than after.

  1. Route scope on multi-segment ticketsDo market rules match on ticket-level origin and destination, or per coupon with the base fare prorated from the fare calc? This may legitimately differ per carrier — I need to know if any of your contracts are explicit about it.
  2. How commission actually arrivesIs it netted out at ARC settlement when the agent reports it, or claimed from carriers by invoice after the fact? This decides what "actual" means in §9 and how a claim is generated.
  3. Overrides and PLBFront-end percentages only, or are there target-based overrides and back-end bonuses? PLB needs period accrual, which is a meaningful addition.
  4. CurrencyAll USD, or are there tickets sold and settled in other currencies? Multi-currency is cheap to design in now and expensive to retrofit.
  5. Sub-agent settlement cadenceWeekly alongside ARC, or monthly? And are fees netted against commission on one statement, or invoiced separately?
  6. ScaleTickets per week, number of carrier contracts, number of sub-agents. This decides whether Phase 4 is a batch job or a queue.
  7. Print-stream sampleA capture from the Server Pro integration. The adapter is fixed-position parsing and cannot be specified from description alone.
What unblocks Phase 1

One real carrier contract, one sub-agent agreement, and twenty to fifty tickets with the amounts your team computed by hand — including at least a few refunds and exchanges, and a few your team found genuinely hard. Those become the golden fixtures, and they are worth more to the build than any answer above.
